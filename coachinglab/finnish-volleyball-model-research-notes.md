## 1. The 2024 volleyball-serve CLA study — read in full, citation corrected

**Source:** "Applying the constraints-led approach to facilitate exploratory learning of the volleyball serve," *Journal of Sports Sciences* (2024), DOI 10.1080/02640414.2024.2345415. **Read:** full text.

**Design:** 40 male high-school volleyball-program students (3 age bands, ages ~13–16), counterbalanced crossover: each played a **regulation (1-serve)** game and a **modified (2-serve)** game.

**Findings (verified figures):**
- **Serve speed ↑** in the 2-serve game: M = **60.3 km/h** (2-serve) vs **55.6 km/h** (regulation); significant main effect, F(1,37)=30.20, p<.001.
- **Jump-topspin % ↑** overall: **44.5%** vs **25.2%** — *but* the interaction was significant and the follow-up showed the increase was driven by the **middle age group only** (not junior or senior). So "players attempt harder serves" is real but not uniform.
- **Accuracy ↓** in the 2-serve game (significant main effect, p=.04). The riskier first serve cost accuracy — as hypothesised.
- **Serve-speed variability: NO change** (F=0.004, p=.95). Their hypothesis that variability would rise was **not** supported.
- **Mechanism = removing a rate limiter (fear/anxiety).** 80% of players reported the second serve boosted confidence or reduced pressure; 82.5% reported *intending to explore* (more aggressive/riskier/targeted serve). This is the strongest, cleanest part of the paper and the part worth leaning on.

**What this licenses us to claim (and not):**
- ✅ A task-constraint (2nd serve) that lowers fear *invites exploration* and more ambitious serving under game conditions.
- ❌ NOT "improves accuracy" (it lowered it), ❌ NOT "increases movement variability" (no effect found).
- Caveat to keep: single session, male students, outcome measures (speed/type/accuracy), not a retention/learning test.

**Draft change:** rewrote design-guide **Case A** to state the mechanism (fear↓ → exploration↑, harder first serves) and to *drop* the false accuracy/variability claims. Added the "accuracy actually dropped" point as an honest design note.

## 2. The CLA effectiveness debate — primary studies, with the nuance

Engaged the comparative motor-learning evidence directly rather than via review-speak.

**Gray (2018), baseball opposite-field hitting (CLA vs Differential Learning vs Prescriptive Instruction).** *Read: abstract + synthesis.* 6-week VR study. **CLA was the only group to improve on *both* outcome (points) and action-selection**, and showed **greater functional variability** at the contact point. DL improved points but not selection; PI improved selection but not points. Gray's reading: CLA worked because it developed *both* the coordination *and* the perception of the affordance. → Best single citation that CLA can out-perform both a pure self-organisation method (DL) and a technique-first method (PI) on transfer-relevant measures.

**Moy, Renshaw & Pavey (2020), hurdling, PE students.** *Read: full PDF.* A **single 40-minute** constraints-led lesson significantly improved hurdling technique vs control — useful as "even brief CLA exposure moves a complex coordination skill." Authors themselves note empirical CLA evidence is still thin.

**Chapman (QUT thesis), cricket spin bowling.** *Read: abstract/synthesis.* Both traditional and CLA improved spin rate; **CLA better overall, especially for younger/less-skilled** — but **traditional was better for the *more skilled* bowlers.** Important honesty: the picture is *not* "CLA always wins"; skill level moderates it.

### The whole comparative literature, distilled (Gray's running log + reviews)

Rather than lean on one paper, this distils **Gray's running tally of direct head-to-head training studies** (*Comparative Training Studies of Traditional Prescriptive Coaching and "Ecological" Approaches*, perceptionaction.com/comparative) — 21 studies as of 2025, spanning CLA, differential learning (DL), and nonlinear pedagogy (NLP) vs traditional/prescriptive — plus two 2025 systematic reviews.

**The pattern across ~21 studies:**

- **When there is a difference, it almost always favours the variability/ecological approach** — across sports: shot put, speed skating, soccer, baseball, futsal, golf, basketball, volleyball, tennis, weightlifting. Effects show up most on **transfer-relevant measures**: adaptability, retention, decision/affordance perception, solution variety, creativity, and **injury-risk markers** (Orangi soccer/basketball, Ghanti jump-landing — lower ACL-risk kinematics).
- **A sizeable minority show *no significant difference* on the primary outcome** (Lee 2014 tennis; Hossner 2016 ×2; Lindsay 2022 weightlifting; Domenico 2023 CMJ; Deuker 2023 soccer). Crucially, **even the "no difference" studies usually add something for the eco group** — more solution variety/degeneracy (Lee), better performance in the more-variable test version (Domenico), better retention (Deuker). **Almost none favour the traditional/prescriptive group.**
- **The "fundamentals first" claim is specifically not supported.** Two studies directly test "drill the decomposed basics first" vs "learn it whole/representative":
  - **Deuker et al. (2023), soccer** — decomposed dribbling/passing "fundamentals" drills vs learning them through small-sided games: **no significant difference, some better retention for the games.** (This is the article behind Gray's podcast ep. 455, "Do We Really Need to Learn the 'Fundamentals'… First?")
  - **Caldeira et al. (2023), volleyball** — CLA practice of the **spike against an opposing block** produced a higher % of successful attacks than traditional/blocked practice (functional movement variability). *(Not a pure simplification-vs-decomposition study — corrected from an earlier "blocking" mislabel.)*
  - This is the empirical heart of Gray's *Advanced Ecological Approach* (2025) **Ch. 7 — "Adjusting the 'Dials'… the Better Alternative to a 'Fundamentals First' Approach"**: the alternative to decomposing-to-basics is **task simplification** — scale the *whole* skill (lighter/softer ball, lower net, smaller court, fewer players, slower feed) while keeping perception and action coupled — not break it into closed parts.
- **Systematic reviews converge:** the 2025 *Sports Medicine – Open* review of team-invasion sports found NLP **≥** linear pedagogy for **technical** skill (mostly no difference, several favour NLP, none favour linear) and NLP **>** linear for **tactical** skill.

**The honest counter-current (keep this in the model's "humility" line):**

- **Effects are moderated.** Newer soccer work (Yang 2025; Frontiers 2025) finds NLP's advantage is **mediated/moderated by learner *adaptability*** — high-adaptability learners gain most from exploratory training; some learners benefit from more structured consolidation. And Chapman's cricket-spin thesis found **traditional better for the *already-skilled***, CLA better for younger/less-skilled. So "eco always wins for everyone" is an overclaim.
- **Method quality is mixed:** small samples, short interventions, heterogeneous outcome measures and sports; proponents (Renshaw, Chow) concede the empirical base is still thin.

**Net for our model:** the strong, defensible claims are **(1)** decomposing to "teach the basics first" has **no empirical advantage** over learning the whole skill scaled down (and often costs transfer/retention) — which directly undercuts the §1 / *lajilinjaukset* technique-first default; **(2)** the right way to make things easier for beginners is **task simplification, not decomposition**; **(3)** the eco/variability approach is at worst equal and usually better on the measures that matter for a game (adaptability, decisions, retention), with a bonus on injury-risk markers. We pair these with the humility caveat (moderators + study quality) rather than selling a silver bullet.

**Draft change:** rewrote §7 evidence to lead with the distilled pattern + the fundamentals-first finding; added the task-simplification-not-decomposition principle to §7 and the Practice Design Guide; sharpened §1's technique-first bullet to say fundamentals-first is *not empirically supported*; added Gray (2018), Moy et al. (2020), Caldeira et al. (2023), Deuker et al. (2023), the comparative log, and the 2025 SMO review to §13.

## 3. The federation's own documents — read closely (the most important find)

### 3a. The *lajilinjaukset* (skill guidelines) — primary source for the §1 critique

**Sources read (full):** `Lentopallo-Lajilinjaukset.pdf` (29 pp.) and `Lapsuusvaiheen-Lajilinjaukset.pdf` (11 pp.), Suomen Lentopalloliitto.

**Structure:** every skill (serve variants, attack, reception, set, block, defence) is presented as **Periaatteet (principles) + Opetusavaimet (teaching keys)** — i.e. a set of prescribed movement cues to install.

**The "one correct technique" tell (verbatim, recurring):** the **first principle on almost every page** is some form of:
> *"Yksinkertaiset liikkeet ovat tehokkaampia ja toistettavampia kuin monimutkaiset."*
> (Simple movements are more effective and more repeatable than complex ones.)

This is a single-ideal-technique credo, and it's the organising assumption. Footwork is fully scripted — e.g. attack approach *"askelpituus: pieni, suurempi, SUURIN, pienin"*, *"askel, askel, heitto, askel, askel + hyppy"* for the jump-float serve, and pages of exact step sequences for transition attacks by position and handedness.

**But — and this is the fair part — the docs do fold in contemporary knowledge:**
- **External-focus / outcome cues** (Wulf-style): *"Pallo tottelee käsien kulmaa"* (the ball obeys the angle of the arms); *"Kulmaa kädet kohti kohdetta."*
- **Game-reading / perception content:** *"Näe peli: pallo – passari – pallo – hyökkääjä"*; *"mitä pidempään katsot passaria… sitä nopeammin pystyt reagoimaan"*; peripheral vision for the attacker (*"perifeerinen katse"*).
- **One genuinely affordance/decision line (the strongest bit):** *"Hyökkääjällä on paljon tapoja tehdä pisteitä. Tärkein tehtävä on tunnistaa tilanne ja etsiä keino tehdä piste."* (The attacker has many ways to score; the main task is to read the situation and find a way.)

**The precise critique (the user's, sharpened):** the documents *try* to be contemporary, but the contemporary elements are **bolted onto a one-true-technique chassis** rather than driving it. External-focus cues and "the ball obeys your arms" are good and eco-D-compatible — yet they sit beside fully prescribed footwork and "simple = repeatable" as the master principle. The result is the same incoherence we flagged for the basketball models (§3 of the draft): a modern vocabulary layered over an install-the-template pedagogy. Mixing external cues with prescriptive templates is a milder cousin of the IP+eco-D blend Gray warns against — the cue philosophy and the template philosophy pull in different directions.

### 3b. The encouraging counter-find: eco-D is *already* in federation coach education

The **Lasten lentopallovalmennus (LLV, U9–U13)** course page states the course covers *"miten mm. ekologisen dynamiikan periaatteita voidaan soveltaa käytännön valmennukseen"* (how the principles of ecological dynamics can be applied in practical coaching). Source: <https://www.lentopallo.fi/koulutukset/lasten-lentopallovalmennus/>.

**Why this matters a lot:** the federation **already endorses ecological dynamics in its U9–U13 coach course**, while its written *lajilinjaukset* remain technique-template-driven. So there is an **internal tension inside the federation itself** — modern theory in the coach course, one-true-technique in the skill guidelines. Our model isn't importing an alien idea; it's offering to **resolve a contradiction the federation already embodies**, and to carry the eco-D commitment the LLV already makes through into the skill guidelines and session design. This is the single best framing lever for adoption.

**Draft change:** rewrote §1 (technique-first bullet) and §2 (what exists / the gap) to cite the *lajilinjaukset* directly, name the bolted-on-contemporary nuance fairly, and add the "eco-D already in LLV" point as the opportunity. Added both PDFs and the LLV page to §13.

## 4. Volleyball-specific perception–action / expertise research

Searched and skimmed the volleyball expertise literature (mostly abstracts + one in-situ paper).

**What the literature shows:**
- **Experts use fewer, longer fixations** and extract more per fixation (Castellar/Piras et al., *Response Time, Visual Search…*, PMC4021845; Schorer/PLOS One 2024 gaze-dynamics study). Setters/experts **anticipate set direction** from early kinematic information (Scientific Reports 2020, s41598-020-74487-x).
- **Representative design matters — and changes the findings.** A spiking-defence study (PMC10968438) found visual-search behaviour **differs between off-court video and on-court (head-mounted) perspectives** — i.e. lab video under-represents the real task. An **in-situ** 6v6 backcourt-defence study (JSSM 11(2):339) found **highly-skilled players used *more* exploratory visual search**, fixating *more* locations and attending to **functional spaces** (between attackers/blockers), not just the ball.

**Honest caveat (important):** most of this body is **perceptual-cognitive** expertise research — video occlusion, eye-tracking, reaction keys — a tradition that often carries information-processing assumptions. So it isn't straightforwardly "eco-D evidence." What *does* transfer to our argument is narrower and defensible: **(1) representative/in-situ tasks reveal different (and richer) perception than decomposed lab tasks** — direct support for representative learning design; **(2) skilled volleyball perception is about reading information across the net (server, setter, hitter, spaces), not executing a closed motor pattern** — support for "reads, not just reps."

**Draft change:** add a short, honestly-bounded note to §7 ("the perception-action engine") and a couple of citations to §13. Do **not** overclaim this as eco-D proof.

## 8. Technique vs skill across the literature (esp. eco-D / nonlinear pedagogy)

A focused pass on how the field defines *technique* vs *skill*. The single most useful finding: **there are two different technique/skill distinctions in circulation, and they are not compatible.** Getting this straight matters for the model because the federation's *lajilinjaukset* (and the basketball models' decision layer) sit in the first one, while our stance (§4) commits to the second.

### 8a. The headline: two incompatible distinctions

- **(A) The traditional / coaching distinction.** *Technique* = the movement pattern; *skill* = choosing and applying the **right technique at the right time**. Skill is technique **plus** decision-making/selection, stacked on top. This is the standard coaching-education line — and it is quietly **information-processing**: it assumes a perceive → *select the technique* → execute sequence, with execution (technique) separable from and prior to the decision. It also implies you can/should build the technique first, then add the deciding.
- **(B) The ecological-dynamics / nonlinear-pedagogy distinction.** *Skill* = the **adaptive, functional relationship between performer and environment** (attunement to affordances; finding a functional movement solution to *this* situation). *Technique* = merely **one coordination solution**, not a building block to be installed first, and **not separable** from perceiving and deciding. Decision and execution are one coupled act, not two layers.

The practical upshot: the model can't endorse "skill = the right technique at the right time" without smuggling IP back in (the very incoherence §4 rules out). We use distinction **(B)**.

### 8b. Traditional / coaching definitions (distinction A)

- **Guthrie (1952):** skill = "the ability to bring about some end result with maximum certainty and minimum outlay of energy, or of time and energy."
- **Knapp (1963):** "Skill is the **learned ability to bring about predetermined results with maximum certainty**, often with the minimum outlay of time, energy, or both." *(Note the giveaway words: "predetermined results," "maximum certainty" — a repeatability/consistency framing.)*
- **Coaching-education shorthand** (e.g. brianmac, BÚ Soccer Coach Weekly, Rob Ellis): *technique* = the basic movement (can be "open" or "closed"); *skill* = "the ability to **choose and perform the proper technique at the right time**, successfully, regularly, and with minimal effort," often written as **SKILL = ABILITY + TECHNIQUE.**
- This camp explicitly treats technique as the raw material and "skill" as its intelligent application — coherent on its own terms, but built on the perceive-then-execute split.

### 8c. Bernstein — the historical bridge to (B)

Bernstein's *dexterity* is where the field starts dissolving the technique/skill split:

- **Dexterity = "the ability to find a motor solution for any external situation — that is, to adequately solve any emerging motor problem correctly, quickly, rationally, and resourcefully."** (Bernstein, *On Dexterity and Its Development*) — and the metaphor: "the ability to **create a perfect key for any emerging lock**."
- **"Repetition without repetition"** — practice is "repeatedly solving a constantly evolving motor problem with techniques that are changed and perfected from repetition to repetition." Dexterity "is **not a property of movement per se**" but of the actor's capacity to flexibly generate adapted solutions.
- This is the root of the eco-D claim that there is no one ideal technique, and that skill ≠ a stored pattern.

### 8d. Newell — coordination → control → skill

- Newell (1985), *Coordination, Control and Skill*: **coordination** = assembling a movement pattern from the body's redundant degrees of freedom; **control** = parameterising/scaling that pattern to the task; **skill** = **optimising** the coordination-and-control solution for the performance context. Learning = **search in the perceptual-motor workspace** for stable, functional solutions (Newell, Kugler, van Emmerik, McDonald). This is the constraints-model lineage already in §7 of the model — "technique" is just an early, partial point in that search, not the destination.

### 8e. The ecological-dynamics / NLP reframing (distinction B)

- **Araújo & Davids (2011), "What exactly is acquired during skill acquisition?"** — skill acquisition "may not refer to an entity but rather to **the emergence of an adaptive, functional relationship between an organism and its environment**." They flag the field's **"organismic asymmetry"** (over-locating skill *inside* the athlete) and prefer the terms **"skill adaptation"** or **"skill attunement."**
- **Gray** (the definition Sarama quotes in *Transforming Basketball*): **"Skill is the ability of a player to use information from the environment to find and execute a movement solution."** Paired with his **action capacity vs skill** distinction: *action capacity* = the movements you can physically produce; *skill* = using information to deploy them to solve the problem (expanding the field of affordances).
- **Crisp eco-D pair** (skilledathleticism.com, summarising the eco-D literature): *Technique* = **"any coordination pattern … applied to the performance of a specific motor action"**; *Skill* = **"the adaptation of one's technique to produce functional and beneficial outcomes in contexts that require them."**
- **Enskilment** (Woods, Rudd, Davids et al., 2021): skill as a **progressive attunement** between an athlete's action capabilities and the affordances of the environment — learning as **education of attention**, not internalising a template.
- **Sarama, *Transforming Basketball* (2024):** "Skills are **not** actions or techniques… they are the relationship between the player and their environment," and "there is no 'one way' to perform a skill because what is functional for one player could be very different for another."

### 8f. The honest nuance (don't overclaim)

- Even eco-D-friendly practitioners (e.g. skilledathleticism) note **technique training isn't "wrong"** — an athlete's action is never wholly new, and isolated coordination work (or video feedback) *can* widen the movement repertoire. The objection is to (i) the **organismic asymmetry** — over-investing time in the athlete-side at the expense of task/environment, and (ii) treating one technique as **the** solution or as a **prerequisite** to play. So "technique" keeps a minor, deliberately-bounded role; it just isn't the foundation or the definition of skill.

### 8g. Why this matters for the model

- It sharpens the **§1 critique**: the *lajilinjaukset*' "Opetusavaimet + simple-is-repeatable" is distinction **(A)** in action — technique as the unit, "reading the game" bolted on top. Naming the two distinctions lets us say *precisely* what we're changing.
- It reinforces **§4 coherence**: adopting eco-D means adopting definition **(B)** of skill all the way down — so we should avoid the seductive coaching cliché "skill is the right technique at the right time," which re-imports the IP split.
- It gives the model a **one-line, citable definition of skill** to lead with (Gray / Araújo & Davids), plus the Bernstein "perfect key for any lock" image for coach-facing material.

## 9. Finnish youth competition rules — the game kids actually play (correcting §10)

**Why this section exists:** the draft's §10 "Active Start + FUNdamentals (~3–8)" originally said the stage had **"0% net game"** and listed **"a real net game"** under *Not yet*. That is **factually wrong** for Finland and contradicts the model's own anti-decomposition thesis. Finnish kids play real, constrained net matches from ~6–8. Sources: Junnulentis **Minisäännöt 2025-2026** (<https://junnulentis.fi/2025/08/14/minisaannot-2025-2026/>); Lentopalloliitto mini-ikäisten säännöt (<https://www.lentopallo.fi/valmennus/mini-ikaisten-valmennus/mini-ikaisten-saannot-ja-pelimuodot/>); PowerCup säännöt/sarjat (<https://www.powercup.fi/turnaus/saannot/>, <https://www.powercup.fi/sarjat/>).

**Petter Nilsson (SE) — the catch is a design tension, not an endorsement.** Nilsson's actual position is **stronger/opposite** to the Finnish catch rule: he advocates **removing the allowed catch (*koppi*) altogether to make the gameplay *more* representative** — the catch keeps rallies alive but breaks the continuous perception–action coupling that makes a contact game-like. His argument is that the *other* constraints (lighter/slower ball, lower net, smaller court, no forced underhand pass) are enough to keep the real 2v2 game playable for the very young **without** a de-representing crutch. So the honest framing for the model is a genuine trade-off: **the catch buys participation/accessibility; removing it buys representativeness.** Flagged in §10 as **[DECIDE]**. (Earlier draft wrongly paraphrased Nilsson as merely "no need to just dribble/catch" — corrected.)

### 9a. The mini-game ladder (U9–U13)

- **Age naming** is moving to international: **U9 (F), U11 (E), U13 (D)**, U15 (C), U17 (B), U20 (A).
- **U9 (F) — MiniLentis, 2 vs 2.** Court rec. 4,5 × 4,5 m (length can extend to ~9 m; two games per net), **net 160 cm (±10)**, light ball (Mikasa SKV5 / VS170W). Two sets to 15 (point cap; match can end 1–1). Two-tier: **Tiikeri (beginners) / Super (experienced)**.
- **U11 (E) — 3 vs 3.** Badminton-court width (6,1 m), **net 180 cm**.
- **U13 (D) — 3 vs 3** (badminton-court doubles lines, **net 200 cm**); the D regional championship is now nationally unified at **3v3**. 6v6 also possible (net 200).

### 9b. The constraint-led design *baked into the rulebook* (this is the important find)

The youth rules are, in effect, a constraints-led / representative-design system the federation already runs:

- **Catches allowed (*koppi*) to keep rallies alive (U9):** Tiikeri = two catches (1st and 2nd touch); Super = one catch. After a catch the player may move to the net; play continues from the player's own throw as a set/forearm pass. Spiking is allowed if not catch-assisted. **(This is the most *debatable* of the design choices — see the Nilsson note above: the catch trades representativeness for accessibility. The rest of the list is closer to clean representative-design-by-rule.)**
- **Movement with the ball (U9) — NEW 2025-26:** *"lapsi saa (lue pitää) liikkua pallo kädessä."* Explicitly loosens the carry rule so small kids can actually play.
- **Triplapallo (Triple Ball) — NEW last season, U11/U13 Tiikeri series.** Play runs in **three-ball cycles**: (1) serve, (2) coach-fed "jatkopallo" to the receiving team, (3) coach-fed ball to the serving team. Adult feeds two-handed underhand, above antenna height, straight to a player, from outside the 3 m line; team **aims for three-touch control (reception → set → attack)**; every ball = 1 point. **Explicit stated goal: "opettaa kolmen kosketuksen pallonhallintaan."** This is representative-design-by-rule — it *guarantees* the coupled reception→set→attack chain the model wants, at high tempo, without a closed drill.
- **Free reception — NEW:** fingers *or* forearm allowed (U11/U13), i.e. the rules **stop mandating one "correct" platform** — directly counter to the lajilinjaukset's one-technique tilt.
- **Underhand serve scaffolding (U9 Tiikeri):** first serve must be underhand; a failed first serve may be retried as a two-handed underhand throw — removes the serve bottleneck so rallies happen.
- **Tie-break-style rotating serve** (every 2 points serve switches + rotation) — keeps everyone serving and rotating.
- **Two difficulty tiers everywhere — Tiikeri/Super (PowerCup: Tsemppi/Super)** — same age, different rule difficulty, so the *constraints* scale to the player rather than the player being sorted out.

### 9c. Consequence for the model

- **Reframes the central tension (§1/§2).** It is not just "eco-D in the LLV course vs technique-template in the lajilinjaukset." The **game design itself is already constraint-led and representative**; the lag is in the **written skill guidelines and inherited coach habits**. So the model is *even more* a "carry the good thing you already do through to skill coaching" pitch, and *less* an import. Strong adoption lever.
- **Kills the "0% net game / not yet a real net game" framing in §10.** Rewrote the Active Start bullet: constrained 2v2 net play and ball-taming **coexist** from ~6–8; taming is not a prerequisite gate. Rewrote Learn to Train to name the actual ladder (MiniLentis 2v2 → 3v3, Tiikeri/Super, Triplapallo, free reception) as representative-design-by-rule.
- **Triplapallo is a ready-made worked example** for the Practice Design Guide (a rule that guarantees the coupled chain) — candidate to add there later.
